Executive Playing Summary

Brodie Spencer is a versatile, athletic right-sided defender whose principal strengths are his mobility, defensive intensity, adaptability and ability to operate in several positions.

At just 22, he already plays like a defender several levels above his experience. Nearly 100 senior appearances across the Championship, League One and Scottish Premiership, plus 23 Northern Ireland caps, banked before most full-backs have even found a settled role — and the underlying numbers explain why Oxford United moved to sign him permanently.

Brodie ranks in the 99th percentile for defensive duels won and 98th for defensive actions among his peers — elite numbers at any level, especially for a 22-year-old. Add genuine composure on the ball, a real aerial presence, and the ability to carry the ball forward, and you have a player who is still adding layers to an already outstanding game, who has no ceiling and his data supports this assessment.

Positional flexibility

Progressive potential

Substantial senior experience already accumulated, while retaining considerable scope for development.

International experience

Significant senior Northern Ireland involvement at a young age. He plays as a right or left wing-back in a 3-5-2, or as a right-back when transitioning to a 4-4-2, and has also played centre-back when called upon.

Tactical profile

Brodie is particularly suited to a high-intensity, transition-oriented team where the full-back is expected to defend large spaces and contribute aggressively from wide areas. He also offers an option as a right-sided centre-back in a back three or four, where his mobility, aggression and ability to defend space become particularly important.

In short, he is a multi-functional defensive player: primarily RB/RWB, with the ability to become a wide CB when the tactical shape and structure require it.

He is an adaptable defender rather than a specialist in only one position. He can operate across the defensive line, though his main position has been full-back, where he can be trusted on either the right or left side.

Tactical Roles

How he fits different systems

Three roles he has filled at senior level, and what each gets out of him.

Traditional right-back

Best systems: 4-2-3-1, 4-3-3 or 4-4-2

His 2025/26 Championship experience is significant: he started 29 league games for Oxford, predominantly at right-back.

Role responsibilities
  • Defend 1v1 against the opposition winger or full-back.
  • Press aggressively when the ball enters his zone.
  • Read the game and step forward to intercept passes into the wide channel.
  • Overlap when possession is established and at key in-game trigger points.
  • Underlap and invert into midfield when the winger holds width and a high line.
  • Tuck inside when the opposite full-back pushes forward.
Pros
  • Strong 1v1 mentality — willing to engage fully rather than fall back.
  • Tackling and duel ability suits a side that wants its full-backs to defend aggressively.
  • Can play either side, with significant experience across different defensive positions.
  • Athleticism gives him the capacity to recover if the first defensive action fails.
  • Can move from RB or LB into a back three during build-up.

Right wing-back

Best systems: back three — 3-5-2 or 3-4-3

Playing in front of three centre-backs allows him to:

Role responsibilities
  • Press the opposition full-back.
  • Close down a winger receiving between the lines.
  • Recover into the back five.
  • Attack the outside channel.
  • Carry the ball forward.
  • Arrive late rather than starting every attack from a deep RB position.
Pros
  • More freedom to attack.
  • Defensive aggression is better protected by the three centre-backs.
  • His versatility becomes particularly effective.
  • Good option for a high-energy pressing team.
  • Effectively becomes a fifth defender without the ball.
  • Left wing-back experience lets a manager change shape in-game without losing performance level.

Centre-back

Best systems: 3-4-3, 3-5-2, or emergency CB in a back four

An adaptable supporting position. He spent significant periods at Huddersfield playing CB and LB despite being a natural right-back.

Where his qualities translate
  • Tackling, aggression and mobility.
  • Willingness to defend forward.
  • Ability to cover wide areas — particularly interesting as the right-sided CB in a back three.
Pros
  • Excellent mobility for a wide CB.
  • Can step out onto opposition forwards.
  • Can cover the channel behind an attacking wing-back.
  • Comfortable defending wide areas.
  • Gives the team a natural transition from CB to RB.
  • Useful against two forwards who drift into the channels.

That ability to change team shape and structure without substituting him is arguably one of his biggest tactical assets.

Transition Defending

He defends aggressively after losing possession

When he gets into a defensive duel he is very willing and capable of engaging the ball-carrier, rather than relying exclusively on positional containment. A second 2025/26 dataset records 4.44 ball recoveries, 2.34 tackles won and 1.34 interceptions per 90, with a 58% overall duel-success rate.

His biggest strength: recovering into a duel

Very high tackle and ground-duel numbers suggest the final part of that sequence is a strength. He won 171 of 294 ground duels (roughly 58%) and was dribbled past only 18 times in 2,147 minutes.

That is particularly impressive because an aggressive full-back is naturally more exposed to defensive situations. He competes, and then recovers.

Second-phase defending

1.34 int/90 + 3.52 tkl/90 + 7.17 ground duels won/90

That combination is very compatible with a team that wants to defend proactively — he gets involved earlier. It makes him well suited to a Championship side that wants to:

  • hold a relatively high defensive line;
  • press immediately after losing possession;
  • push its full-backs forward;
  • use the full-back aggressively against wide players;
  • defend transitions with counter-pressure rather than simply retreating.
